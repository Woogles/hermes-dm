Brackenmere was built beside a lake that has gradually receded over the past century. Its people tell stories about a bell tower swallowed by the water long ago. The bell was supposedly rung only when the village faced a danger everyone needed to remember.

The lake recently drained after an unusually dry summer. Beneath it lies a stone causeway and a buried bell tower. The bell’s sound does not erase memories outright: it draws them into a sleeping presence sealed below the tower. The presence is neither wholly malicious nor safe. It is trying to remember its own name.

The old warning bell was made by villagers who feared a disaster they could not prevent. Their descendants forgot the disaster, but the bell kept its purpose.
