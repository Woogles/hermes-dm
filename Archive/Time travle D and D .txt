Assuming you mean **D&D 5.5e / the 2024 rules**, here’s a time-travel campaign framework you can run.

# **A Shattered Century**  
**A 5.5e time-travel campaign**

## Campaign premise

The party discovers that the town they start in, **Harlowe**, is not one town but the same town caught in **overlapping time loops**. A damaged artifact called the **Chronal Loom** lets them move between eras, but every change they make creates a new paradox. The town’s past, present, and future are in conflict:

- In **The Founding**, the town was built peacefully.
- In **The Bloom**, it is healthy and prosperous.
- In **The Ashfall**, it is dying.
- In **The Drowned**, it is underwater.
- In **The Unwritten**, it is being rewritten by time-obsessed rebels.

The party must repair the Loom, stop timeline collapse, and decide how to restore—or protect—century.

This campaign is designed for **levels 1–12**, but can be compressed or stretched.

---

## 5.5 rule integration

Use the **2024 core rules** for normal D&D:

- Ability checks
- Saving throws
- Spell slots and spellbooks
- Advantage/disadvantage
- Actions, Bonus Actions, Reactions, Magic as an Action where applicable
- Exhaustion
- Proficiency bonus progression
- Long rest and short rest structure, as your table uses

Time travel is handled as **optional campaign rules**, not a replacement for 5.5 combat or spellcasting.

---

# Core time travel rules

## 1. The Chronal Loom

The Chronal Loom is the campaign’s time-travel mechanism.

The party can use it to move between linked eras.

### Loom charges

Each era jump costs **Loom charges**.

| Level range | Charges per long rest | Maximum era span |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–4 | 2 charges | 1 era |
| 5–9 | 3 charges | 2 eras |
| 10–12 | 4 charges | 3 eras |

Extra charges can be gained from **Paradox Shards**: 3 shards recharge 1 Loom charge.

This prevents unlimited time hopping while still making travel meaningful.

---

## 2. Time anchors

You cannot safely arrive in a random dead year. You must arrive at a **Time Anchor**: a fixed location that exists in multiple eras.

Examples:

- The Market of Miracles, present and past
- The Old Dam, found in the Bloom and Ashfall eras
- The Clockwork Vaults, underground in every era
- The Ashfall Shrine, future cult site

If there is no anchor, travel to that era requires **3 charges** and triggers an automatic paradox.

---

## 3. Safe travel rules

If multiple copies of the same character exist in the same era, they create a **Temporal Paradox**.

Use this condition:

> **Chronal Stasis**  
> The duplicated character has disadvantage on Wisdom and Charisma checks until they are separated or the paradox is resolved.

The older copy is the **Anchor Copy**. The newer copy is the **Echo Copy**.

If not resolved, the Echo Copy risks being erased from existence at the end of a long rest.

---

## 4. Altering history

A major historical change triggers a **Paradox Die**.

Use a d6:

| Result | Effect |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Catastrophic: the era branches. A faction gains +2 Influence, and the party must repair the paradox or lose access to one era for a session. |
| 2–3 | Major paradox: a faction reacts strongly. The party must spend resources or make a skill challenge. |
| 4–5 | Minor paradox: timeline shifts slightly, but a visible NPC or location changes. |
| 6 | Stable: the change is contained. |

The DM describes the immediate visible consequence, then uses the result to escalate or stabilize the campaign.

---

## 5. Repairing paradoxes

Use 5.5 skill checks.

| Paradox severity | Recommended check | DC |
|---|---|---:|
| Minor | Arcana / History / Investigation | 13 |
| Major | Arcana / Investigation / Religion | 18 |
| Catastrophic | Arcana / History / Religion, with one helper allowed | 23 |

A failure does not erase progress; it worsens consequences or costs Loom charges.

---

## 6. Temporal exposure

Repeated use of the Loom causes strain.

Track **Temporal Exposure** per character.

| Exposure level | Effect |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No HP regained from Short Rests until the next Long Rest. |
| 2 | Disadvantage on Initiative and Dexterity checks. |
| 3 | Treat as 1 Exhaustion. |
| 4 | Treat as 2 Exhaustion. |

Exposure increases by 1 when a character travels more than one era at a time, lands without an anchor, or remains in a paradox zone at the end of a Long Rest.

# Faction structure

## 1. The Loomkeepers

They police stable time travel.

- Prefer anchors
- Hate paradoxes
- Reward safe restoration
- Offer repair quests

## 2. The Ashen Choir

A cult of future believers.

- Want to destroy stable timelines
- Worship paradoxes
- Send assassins and future warriors into the past
- Reward chaos

## 3. The Gilded Ledger

A time-aware crime syndicate.

- Steal across timelines
- Exploit anchors
- Buy future information
- Create heists that require time jumps

## 4. The Unwritten

A rebel army from an alternate future.

- They know what has not happened yet
- They send soldiers backward
- They want to rewrite the town’s fate
- They are the main late-game threat

## 5. The Bloom

The natural, prosperous present faction.

- They want stable ecology and water sources
- They fear drought, blight, and paradox storms
- They reward restoration of nature

# Campaign structure

## Act 1: **The Almanac of the Ninth Year**  
**Levels 1–4**

### Goal

Learn that time travel is happening, repair the Loom, and prevent a small paradox.

### Opening quest

The town has a drought. A local archivist notices that the almanac describes rain that never happened.

The party discovers that a **time leak** is pulling water from the past into the present.

### Objectives

1. Find three Loom fragments hidden across the town.
2. Repair the Chronal Loom.
3. Stop one paradox storm.
4. Decide whether to save the mayor or prevent a future flood.

### Time travel problem

They need to go back to a rainstorm in the Bloom or Founding era, then return the water route back to the present.

### Main locations

- The Market of Miracles
- The Old Dam
- The Clockwork Vaults
- The Grave That Was Never Dug

### Main antagonists

- A Gilded Ledger heist cell
- One Ashen Choir assassin, use a reskinned 5.5 humanoid statblock
- Echo duplicates

### Level 4 boss

Use a reskinned 5.5 CR 3–4 caster as **The Echo Warden**.

Its goal: prevent players from carrying future items back into the past.

---

## Act 2: **The Unwritten Century**  
**Levels 5–8**

### Goal

Collect four Paradox Keys, stop future rebels, and stabilize the timeline.

### Central problem

The Unwritten are erasing events that the party has not yet witnessed.

Future rebels send agents back in time to sabotage anchors. The party must decide which changes matter.

### Objectives

1. Recover the Paradox Keys from four factions.
2. Repair one major timeline fracture.
3. Prevent the Unwritten from rewriting the town’s founding.
4. Save one anchor location that may cease to exist.

### Locations

- The Drowned Market
- The Ashfall Shrine
- The Founding Chapel
- The Ledger Vault
- The Clockwork Bridge

### Faction conflicts

The Loomkeepers want to stabilize everything.  
The Unwritten want to destroy stable eras.  
The Gilded Ledger wants to steal future knowledge.  
The Bloom wants nature restored.

### Mid-campaign challenge

Use a skill challenge over 2 days:

- Arcana DC 15: understand the fracture
- History DC 17: find the correct anchor point
- Investigation DC 14: locate the paradox core
- Religion DC 16: calm or manipulate the Ashen Choir

### Level 8 boss

Use a reskinned 5.5 CR 8–9 caster as **The Unwritten Herald**.

Mechanic: it has a “Rewrite” reaction. Once per round, it can reroll one damage roll or skill check against a timeline event.

---

## Act 3: **The Unmaking**  
**Levels 9–12**

### Goal

Enter the Loom’s core, stabilize the century, and choose an ending.

### Central problem

All eras are beginning to overlap:

- One street has four versions of itself.
- A bridge is dry, flooded, and destroyed simultaneously.
- NPCs remember being saved and never saved.
- Weather is paradoxical.

### Objectives

1. Stabilize four anchors.
2. Recover the Unmaking Key.
3. Prevent a catastrophic collapse.
4. Choose an ending.

### Locations

- The Loom Control Room
- The Split Market
- The Ninth Dam
- The Chapel of Two Names
- The Drowned Bridge
- The Ashfall Throne

### Major challenge

Use a 5.5 group skill challenge:

- Arcana DC 20 to bind the Loom
- History DC 20 to identify the true founding event
- Religion DC 22 to silence or empower the Ashen Choir
- Investigation DC 18 to locate the Unmaking Key

One failure is acceptable if the party spends resources.

### Final boss

Use a reskinned 5.5 CR 12 humanoid caster as **The Unwritten King** or **The Loom Paradox**.

Lair actions:

- Force one ally to reroll a Wisdom save
- Reroll one initiative result
- Trigger a minor paradox storm
- Create an Echo duplicate
- Spend a reaction to erase a temporary ally’s future anchor

---

# Endings

The final choice should distinguish between **established timeline facts** and **unresolved paradoxes**.

## 1. Restore the original century

The party restores the founding timeline.

- The town survives in its original form
- Future rebels are erased
- The Unwritten lose most influence
- Any future tech the party brought back becomes unstable and may vanish unless anchored

## 2. Accept a new stable timeline

The party creates a new, clean present.

- Harlowe is saved in a new form
- Some NPCs remember changed events
- The Unwritten are contained but not erased
- The party can keep limited future knowledge safely

## 3. Split the timeline

The party stabilizes multiple futures.

- One timeline becomes a sanctuary
- One becomes a war zone
- One becomes the true original timeline
- The Loom can only travel between sanctioned branches

## 4. Destroy the Loom

The party ends time travel.

- Paradoxes collapse into a single future
- No one can bring future knowledge into the past
- Harlowe remains stable, but all prior faction influence is frozen

# Sample side quests

## 1. The Rain That Never Fell

A farmer remembers rain in 1994, but the town has a drought in 2001.

Time travel problem:

- Go back to 1994
- Find the missing dam gate
- Return it to 2001
- Avoid creating a new flood in the Ashfall era

Paradox risk:

- If the party removes too much water, the Ashfall Shrine loses its water source.
- If they return too much, the present market floods.

---

## 2. The Grave That Was Never Dug

A cemetery has two tombstones for the same person in one era, and one tombstone in another.

Objective:

- Find the original death anchor
- Repair the memory paradox
- Prevent the dead from becoming an uncontrolled echo

5.5 skill challenge:

- History DC 15: identify the true death
- Religion DC 16: calm the restless dead
- Arcana DC 14: stabilize the grave marker

---

## 3. The Ledger Heist of Two Years

The Gilded Ledger stole a vault in 1997 and a second vault in 2003.

Objective:

- Go to 1997 to plant false evidence
- Return to 2003 to erase their suspicion
- Do not let their future copy find your past copy

Anchor rule:

- You cannot bring a 2020 lockpick back to 1997 unless that lockpick type existed at that time.

---

## 4. The Two Mayors

Harlowe has two mayoral records for the same century.

Objective:

- Determine which mayor was real
- Repair the civic anchor
- Prevent the Ashen Choir from crowning the false mayor

Paradox rule:

- One mayor is an Anchor NPC.
- The other is an Echo NPC.
- If the Echo mayor is crowned, the town’s political history destabilizes.

---

# Key locations

## The Market of Miracles

A central marketplace with stalls that exist in multiple eras.

- In The Bloom, it is prosperous.
- In The Drowned, it is partly flooded.
- In The Ashfall, it is burned.
- In The Unwritten, it is being rewritten by rebels.

## The Clockwork Vaults

Underground engine rooms.

- Good for combat
- Good for Loom repair
- Good for paradox cores
- Good for Loomkeepers as allies

## The Old Dam

Water control center.

- Best for the Act 1 drought problem
- Can be altered across three eras
- Should be a major anchor

## The Ashfall Shrine

Future cult site.

- Ashen Choir rituals
- Time storms
- Cursed relics
- Rituals require Religion checks

## The Founding Chapel

Past civic center.

- Civic anchor
- Mayor paradox
- Historical records
- Useful for resolving memory contradictions

## The Split Market

A late-game location where four versions of the same market overlap.

- Environmental hazard
- Paradox storm
- Time-split combat zone

---

# Time travel hazards

## Paradox storms

When an era is unstable, use these effects as optional hazards:

- Creatures gain disadvantage on Wisdom checks
- Spellcasters must succeed on a DC 12 Concentration save when cast on a paradox zone
- Initiative order changes after the first round
- A duplicated NPC may become hostile instead of helpful

## Echo zones

A zone where future beings are visible.

- The party can interact with them
- If the party changes their cause too much, the Echo Copy becomes hostile
- If they are stabilized, they can be removed from the timeline

## Dead years

An era with no anchor.

- Time travel costs 3 charges
- Arrival forces a Paradox Die
- If no rescue is made, the party risks becoming Echo Copies themselves

---

# 5.5 combat integration

Time travel should not change normal 5.5 combat too much.

## Recommended combat approach

- Use standard 5.5 initiative.
- Echo duplicates use normal statblocks, with an Echo token.
- Time magic uses normal spell slots and spellbooks.
- Time effects should be campaign features, not universal combat balance.

## Time effects in combat

Use these sparingly.

### Time Step

**1st-level spell, 5.5 spellcasting compatible**

- Range: self
- Casting time: Bonus Action
- Range: 15 feet
- Effect: teleport to an unoccupied space, gain no speed until the start of your next turn, and gain one Echo Token.

### Rewind

**3rd-level spell, 5.5 spellcasting compatible**

- Casting time: Reaction
- Range: 10 feet
- Trigger: one creature in range fails a saving throw or skill check
- Effect: it can reroll after seeing the failure.
- If the creature is a duplicate, it takes no damage from the spell that triggered the reaction.

Use these only if you want the party to be directly time-capable.

# Optional campaign artifact

## The Chronal Loom

**Wondrous item, requires attunement**

As an Action, spend one Loom Charge to travel to an anchored era.

You can spend Paradox Shards to recharge charges.

While attuned, a character can use:

- **Anchor Sense**: perceive the nearest stable anchor within 100 feet.
- **Echo Sense**: know whether an NPC is an Anchor Copy or Echo Copy.
- **Repair Pulse**: once per Long Rest, grant advantage to one Arcana or History check made to stabilize a paradox.

# How to run each session

## 1. Travel

The party chooses an era and anchor.

Ask:

- What changed?
- What could make the change matter?
- What faction is reacting?
- What resource or time anchor is at risk?

## 2. Arrival

Describe the era.

Use 5.5 skill checks to gather clues:

- Investigation for physical changes
- History for memories
- Arcana for magical/time traces
- Religion for cult reactions
- Nature for weather/ecology if relevant

## 3. Alteration

If the party changes something, roll the Paradox Die.

Do not narrate every consequence in full. Show the visible result first.

Example:

> “You repair the dam gate. The water returns to the present. But the Ashfall Shrine’s water source is now gone.”

## 4. Repair

Use a 5.5 skill challenge.

The party can spend Paradox Shards or Loom charges to reduce DC or gain advantage.

## 5. Update tracks

Record:

- Loom charges used
- Faction influence
- Paradox severity
- Temporal exposure
- Visible timeline changes

---

# What to keep this campaign balanced

Do not let players use time travel as a free win condition.

Use these limits:

- You can only land at an anchor.
- You cannot bring a future item into the past unless it existed there.
- One major paradox should be unresolved at a time.
- If a paradox escalates, faction influence increases.
- If the party solves everything, they must still pay Loom charges.
- Final resolution should require multiple skill challenges and one final encounter.

1. Find the Loom.  
2. Stabilize a water problem.  
3. Repair a small paradox.  
4. Collect Paradox Keys.  
5. Stop future rebels.  
6. Prevent civic rewriting.  
7. Stabilize four anchors.  
8. Enter the Loom.  
9. Choose the final century.

# One-page starting hook

You can give this to the party:

> “Harlowe has a drought, but the town archives say it rained in the past. Worse, the market’s layout changes from year to year. A clockwork device beneath the Clockwork Vaults has three broken anchors. If the device stays damaged, Harlowe will not be one town. It will be four towns trying to become one.”

Then the party begins with:

- An investigation at the Market of Miracles
- A Nature or History check about rain patterns
- A minor combat with one Ledger agent and two Echo Guards
- A skill challenge to locate one Loom fragment
- A travel decision to the Founding or Bloom era

---

# Optional class support

If you want the party to own time travel, use the 2024 spell system:

- Time Step: 1st level
- Rewind: 3rd level
- Echo Sense: as a class feature, not a spell

If you prefer a more balanced approach, make them Loom users rather than full time mages. That keeps 5.5 balance intact.
